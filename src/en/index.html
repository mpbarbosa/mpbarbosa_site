Intro

I spent 26 years building and maintaining telecom billing systems — invoicing, tax, accounting and financial reconciliation, and subscriber base migrations. It is a domain where the software answers for money already charged and obligations already filed: correctness is not a quality detail, it is the product.

Over the past year I brought that discipline to development with AI coding agents. I use Claude Code, GitHub Copilot and Cursor to write most of the code; my job is to decide the architecture, define what has to hold true, and verify every change before it reaches production. Both live products were built this way — with unit tests, end-to-end Playwright specs, and deploy pipelines with a rehearsed rollback.

The underlying work has not changed: it is still engineering supervision. What changed is how fast the code appears, not who is accountable for it.

In production

  • Portal Brasileirão — Companion app for the Brazilian Série A championship

    Standings, fixtures and statistics for the Série A. OAuth login, Material Design 3 tokens and a live /api/health endpoint. Seven GitHub Actions workflows including rollback, with a documented flip-back drill, and keyless deploys over OIDC. When no API token is available the app falls back to a frozen snapshot instead of failing. React 19 · TypeScript · Express 5 · Tailwind 4 · AWS · 45 unit tests and 30 end-to-end Playwright specs · 742 commits in nine days (23–31 August 2026)

  • Agora na Copa 2026 — Companion app for the FIFA World Cup 2026

    Monte Carlo simulator for qualification odds, using a Dixon-Coles model and the Article 13 FIFA tie-breaking criteria applied in the correct order. External integrations (FIFA API, Open-Meteo, Google Trends) each have their own fallback. Served by nginx and systemd. React 19 · TypeScript · Express · AWS · 34 unit tests and 37 end-to-end Playwright specs · 1,279 commits

About

People who understand tax rules, invoicing and reconciliation usually do not build modern software. People who build modern software have rarely reconciled a credit-card system. I spent 26 years on the first side and the past year shipping production products on the second.

In practice that means being able to say why a number is wrong before opening the code — and then fixing the code. It is the combination that matters in billing, reconciliation, revenue assurance, data migration and systems in regulated industries, where the cost of being wrong is not an ugly screen: it is an incorrect charge or an incorrect filing.

Outside of code, I am interested in music — I have a project to compose using Strudel.
